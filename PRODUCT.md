# Product

## Register

product

## Users

First of all the product team: the people who triage incoming bugs and ideas, move them across status columns, set priority and write the reply the author will see. They open the board regularly, often several times a day, on a desktop, and work through it as a queue.

Second, the authors: employees and users of the client product who post through a widget on that product's site, then open the board by link to see what happened to their post and to vote on others. They come rarely and briefly, often from a phone.

The board is generic: every client product gets its own board (the demo project is "Demo"), so nothing in the interface is tied to one product.

## Product Purpose

One place for bugs and ideas instead of scattered messages in chats and threads. The team gets a single triage queue with statuses and priorities; authors get transparency (they see the status of their post and get notified when it is done); votes show which ideas matter most.

Success: the team clears the "new" column quickly, and an author can tell in a few seconds where their post stands.

## Brand Personality

Lively but grown-up: not boring, not high-contrast. A warm tool with humour (the vote buttons are 👍 and 💩 on purpose), built on the company's Rocont green as the one hue. Liveliness comes from the details and the moments of feedback (reactions, votes, small motion, empty states), not from many colours or loud contrast. The board is still a work queue for the team.

## Anti-references

- Generated "AI look": gradient blobs, glass panels, icon-in-a-tinted-circle cards, tiny uppercase letter-spaced labels, dashed hint boxes.
- Dull monochrome admin kits: grey ground, ink-black buttons, no colour anywhere.
- Childish, toy-like colour: an earlier version mixed the company green with purple on a lavender ground and read as "for kids". One hue, not two.
- Trello: saturated multicolour labels and loud boards.
- Harsh contrast for its own sake.

References in the right lane: Linear (density, calm type, precise states), Notion (warm neutrality, readability), Canny and Featurebase (what a feedback board needs to do).

## Design Principles

1. **The queue comes first.** The team's next action (open, triage, move, reply) is always one click away; decoration never competes with it.
2. **Status at a glance.** An author on a phone can read where a post stands in seconds.
3. **Playful where it gives feedback.** Personality lives in reactions, votes, empty states and small moments, not in layout tricks.
4. **Brand colour means something.** Green marks actions and the current selection, so colour on screen always carries a meaning.
5. **Generic by design.** Works for any product's board; no copy or visuals tied to a single client.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: 4.5:1 contrast for text in both themes, visible keyboard focus, every motion has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback, statuses carry a text label and not only a colour.
